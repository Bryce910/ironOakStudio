# Grandstone rendering audit — 2026-11-01

Read-only audit of the native `production-town` / `production-hall` captures, their `profile.json.world.json`, and engine/shader bindings. No GPU runs or renderer mutations were performed for this audit.

## What is visibly wrong

The town image has a very large, mostly empty, nearly white flat foreground. Architectural surfaces and the boulder wall behind them repeat without the publisher's connected terraced composition. The hall is a long, cold dark chamber; its practical light sources do not establish a readable warm sequence, and its ceiling has very little illumination. Close pillars have obvious stretched/smeared surface patterns; this is a material/geometry fidelity issue that raising exposure cannot fix.

## Confirmed omissions and limits

1. **Grandstone has zero local radiance probes.** Both native reports say `local_probes.enabled=true` but `cache_regions=0`, `count=0`, `ready=0`, `capture_calls=0`. `engine/world_demo_probe_capture.inc:3-12` defines exactly two caches and routes only the old village and rainforest to them. Grandstone returns `-1`. The existing local specular and SH diffuse path therefore never contributes here. A third stronghold region with exterior courtyard probes and bounded interior hall/forge probes is a small extension of existing machinery. It will improve material grounding, but is not full GI: the SH blend currently supplies at most 40% for interiors (`engine/world_local_probes.cpp:250`).

2. **The architectural sky-access/diffuse return bake belongs to the old village.** `engine/world_viewer.cpp:1445-1503` constructs its grid from `world_atlas_village_bounds`, uses reference ground around 24 m, and ignores placements outside that rectangle. Grandstone is approximately 1,131 m high at a distant map location. `village_surfaces.directional_sky=true` in telemetry does not mean Grandstone is receiving that bake. Global hemispheric sky fill is still present; the new mountain hall has no matching architectural sky field. A stronghold domain could reuse the existing method, but a single solid footprint for a large open hall would be a poor substitute for the source scene's lighting. Source-scene light/probe data or carefully bounded hall domains are preferable.

3. **Light energy and coverage are incompatible with the exaggerated hall scale.** `tools/build_nature_grandstone.py:255-265` places vaults at scale 2.6, y14.4, making a roughly 47 m high room; the warm sequence is x±8, y6, radius29, power30. The shader's finite inverse-square attenuation is implemented at `projects/valwood_stronghold/shaders/world_foliage.fs.glsl:459-485`. Roof points 41 m above those lights are outside their radius, so receive exactly zero direct light from them. The one ceiling source at z−105, y28, radius40 cannot light the entire 244 m nave. The authoring validation currently caps light power/radius at 40 (`engine/world_studio.cpp:30`), which is sensible for little torch fixtures but prevents physically adequate monumental architectural lighting. Prefer source-scale assemblies and source light placement first; if monumental scale is retained, use documented high-energy architectural light ranges and a restrained room diffuse return rather than a global exposure lift.

4. **The shader lights only eight camera-selected practicals.** `engine/world_studio_view.inc:378-396` ranks by power / horizontal distance squared and submits eight. A 244 m room with 24 authored lights cannot have all its distant illuminated accents visible simultaneously. This is distinct from the source assets being untextured. Baked static architectural light contribution (or source lightmaps, if present and legally/technically usable), emissive flame meshes, and local indirect fill can retain distant accents without increasing per-fragment light count. Simply adding more `light` lines is not sufficient.

5. **Distance limits flatten the far architecture.** Detail normal maps fade from 80 to 125 m in `world_foliage.fs.glsl:706-717`; direct shadows fade 125–160 m and become fully unshadowed at160 m (`world_foliage.fs.glsl:257-260`). The global directional map uses radius160 m (`engine/world_viewer.cpp:4553-4555`) at2048 px in current settings: roughly15.6 cm per texel. In an overlarge room or long courtyard, far buildings therefore lose both mapped relief and cast-shadow definition. A bounded stronghold normal-detail range is feasible after the material fix. Shadow cascades would be a wider project; do not globally enlarge one shadow map, because that reduces its local precision.

6. **The snow shader intentionally collapses most albedo contrast.** `projects/valwood_stronghold/shaders/world_terrain.fs.glsl:523-544` reads scan textures but folds their luminance into a narrow `.055 * (grain - .5)` multiplier. The dominant snow remains bright blue-white with almost all detail carried by tiny normals. Wind ridges fade according to pixel footprint and most scoured patches occur only beyond x±13..24 m, while a flattened construction pad fills most of the current town image. Better authored banks, packed traffic routes, irregular exposed stone, snow buildup at foundations, and source scene landscaping will help more than adding high-frequency grain or sharpening. Keep walking footprint and powder systems intact.

## Features already functioning

The captures are at native1600×900, not 70% resolution. AO/contact lighting ran all280 frames; source reports confirm contact shadows, volumetric clouds, and fog are enabled. HDR bloom resolves correctly (strength0.035). The post shader uses a sensible filmic curve and exactly one sRGB output conversion (`world_post.fs.glsl:14-25`). The engine also already has PBR, tangent normal maps, metal/roughness, local-probe infrastructure, and explicit HDR environment support. However, this document has no HDR environment configured (`studio_tools.hdr_enabled=false`). Adding another generic post-processing pass is not the primary fix.

## Practical implementation order

1. Recover the publisher demo's native module scale, hierarchy, transforms, and light setup; correct material UV fidelity in a representative close-up before rebuilding more area.
2. Establish one connected exterior composition at source scale: stepped architecture, supporting foundations, real cliff integration, foreground path/props, and irregular snow edges. Use the publisher scene as the layout baseline where available.
3. Add a third local-probe region and authored indoor diffuse return/architectural illumination with visible practical sources. Preserve cool exterior / warm interior contrast; do not raise global exposure to rescue the hall because the courtyard snow is already very bright.
4. Compare exact native close-up viewpoints and walking views against reference. Only extend detail/shadow ranges or texture resolution where the corrected scene demonstrates the need. Run native gameplay and performance checks after the art/rendering decisions are stable.

An image matching claim requires fresh native captures of the corrected layout; the current successful collision/FPS rehearsal is not evidence of publisher-level visual fidelity.
