Read-only reinforcement performance audit — 2026-10-26

Scope
Reviewed watch-close-final.json, its CSV/world JSON, the watch update/draw path,
shared pose upload/draw implementation, scenery aliases and reflection calls.
No builds, tests, cooking, native rendering or changes to engine/assets were
started by this audit. Other native profiles were running sequentially.

Measured evidence
The 1,680 post-warmup watch-close samples report 57.85 mean FPS, 24.76 FPS
1% low, 35.261 ms p99 and 47.827 ms maximum. There are 49 samples over the
22.222 ms budget. Of those, 46 fall in frames 385–454 or 1098–1277; the
remaining 1,430 measured frames have three misses. The first burst has 14/70
misses, mean frame 19.363 ms/update 3.665 ms; the second has 32/180,
19.233 ms/update 3.674 ms. Normal spans average approximately 16.85–17.02 ms
per frame and 2.03–2.21 ms update.

The bursts cross several submission/wait locations: 13 updates exceed 10 ms,
three render CPU samples exceed 10 ms, and frame419 spends 28.380 ms in the
editor stage. Example frame399: update26.934 ms; frame1245: prior present
37.888 ms; frame1266: render CPU17.824 ms. GPU elapsed-stage outliers also
occur in different passes (shadow, foliage, terrain and reflection). GPU
stage values are delayed estimates and present_ms belongs to the previous
swap; individual rows do not prove which call caused the delay.

Slow samples concentrate at sweep phases360–599 of the720-frame turn;
phases0–359 and600–719 have no over-budget samples. Reflection is normally
off through much of the first troublesome direction (near-zero reflection
GPU values), so an unconditional new reflection draw is not an adequate
explanation. The near-zero to ~3 ms reflection transition later in the turn
does add ordinary GPU work. Misses are not unusually close to watch pose
boundaries: 8/49 are within one frame of either visible watch actor's pose
boundary versus256/1680 overall (16.3% versus15.2%).

Code findings
world_village_watch_update uses fixed arrays for four actors/eight batches,
has no hot allocations, and only two actors qualify within180 m in this
profile. WorldModelPoseBlend keeps reserved staging and prelinked adjacent
pose VAOs. Changed weights upload placements/weights only (24-byte GPU
instances), not the20,174-triangle pose meshes or textures. Normally this is
two one-instance uploads per frame here, plus an occasional emptied batch;
load/target linking is outside the frame loop. Color, shadow and reflection
reuse those uploaded weights. No synchronous asset loading, uncapped loop,
per-frame pose relinking or supported watch source defect was found.

The four new guards, two mounted models and tools do add real geometry and
approximately48 MiB of unique mipmapped images according to ASSET_IMPORTS.md.
Near/mid assets and eight poses share their image cache ownership; this is
not an eightfold texture upload. Shared upload functions call glBufferData
and glGetError, so a driver wait can be accounted in update. That remains a
possible wait location, not evidence that these tiny new uploads are the
cause. Existing population and pets already make many more such uploads.

Conclusion and useful next isolation
The new watch source is bounded and no actionable source bug is established
by this trace. Retain this failed smoothness result. A controlled same-view
paired run should record system PSI/VRAM/GTT and narrow CPU timestamps around
actor uploads versus other update work, without changing visible quality.
One staged watch-disable diagnostic can isolate its incremental cost, but
must be recorded separately and cannot replace the integrated acceptance
profile. Existing farm-tools-final reports59.52 mean/48.23 low/p9919.917 ms;
it is a different view, so it neither proves nor disproves a watch regression.
