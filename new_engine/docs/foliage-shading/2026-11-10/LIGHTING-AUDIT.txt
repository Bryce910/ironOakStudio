Lighting assessment — 10 November 2026

Assessment: a useful custom raster lighting foundation; the current finished
visual result should not yet be described as AAA quality. AAA is an artistic
and production standard, not a single rendering feature or certification.

Present in the accepted runtime
- GGX/PBR material response, normal and ARM maps, thin-leaf wrap/transmission.
- SH9 diffuse and GGX-filtered, box-projected local reflection probes. The
  measured opening has five ready 128-pixel local probes.
- Filtered directional sunlight shadowing; current default is a 2048 map,
  nine scalar filter samples, and a 125–160 metre fade.
- Local torch/practical lights with enclosure and clipping approximations.
- Screen-space ambient/contact occlusion, volumetric fog and clouds, temporal
  anti-aliasing, HDR bloom, filmic mapping, sRGB output and a color LUT.

Material limits
- Local indirect probes update around loading/destination transitions, rather
  than providing general real-time light bounce around moving objects.
- One sun shadow map must cover the nearby scene; it lacks a set of cascades
  dedicated to preserving fine contact detail across a larger distance.
- The actual local-light telemetry explicitly reports no point-light shadow
  maps. Enclosure approximations cannot reproduce arbitrary moving occluders.
- Actors share the opaque material lighting path. A dedicated skin-scattering
  model and stronger eye/hair presentation are still missing.
- Exposure is authored. There is no general automatic exposure metering.

Observed presentation gaps
The existing fixed captures show dark canopies against a pale sky and fairly
flat town facades/plaza. Repeated ground/grass patterns are also conspicuous;
that is partly a material and scene-dressing problem. The older images render
internally at1024x576, so their softness must not all be blamed on lighting.
The current shader comparison uses1600x900 windows/1280x720 scene rendering.

Recommended visual priorities
1. Calibrate sky fill, sun, exposure and material values in one campsite view
   and one interior; verify both in daylight and at dusk.
2. Improve local indirect light placement/occlusion and believable contact at
   feet, walls, building foundations and foliage roots.
3. Add scalable sun-shadow coverage/detail and a small budget of genuinely
   shadowed nearby practical lights, measuring RX5300 cost as each is enabled.
4. Treat skin, eyes and hair as a separate character lighting/material pass.
