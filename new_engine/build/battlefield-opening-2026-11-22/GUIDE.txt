BATTLEFIELD OPENING — IMPLEMENTATION AND PLAYTEST GUIDE

Status: playable opening; the full normal-input native route is verified.
Functional verification is not an AAA art, animation or performance certification.
No Windows build is part of this change.

THE OPENING
1. Apartment at night: read the bills, turn on the television, select New Game.
2. Transport into The Broken Field. The hero arrives disoriented while archers,
   dueling soldiers, falling fighters, smoke and nearby fires establish the battle.
3. Recover control and run through the wreckage. A soldier blocks the escape.
4. Press X (controller) or Q (keyboard) during the incoming sword's duck prompt.
   Missing the prompt is lethal. Retry begins just before the ambush.
5. Duck into a tackle, then tap A / F to wrestle the sword free. A hold-button
   alternative is available from Pause. Difficulty adjusts the timing/struggle.
6. The hero gains the stolen sword at the stabbing contact. The ownership receipt
   saves once; retry/load cannot duplicate the sword or grant a suit of armor.
7. Clear three live sword encounters while escaping toward the tree line.
8. Cross the creek bridge into the forest and follow the smoke to the travelers.
9. Continue the established camp → stolen provisions → reward → village chapter.
   Camp dialogue recognizes the battlefield escape and the already-owned sword.

CONTROLS
Movement/camera: LS / RS, or WASD / mouse. Shift runs outdoors.
Ambush: X / Q. Struggle: repeated A / F, or enabled hold alternative.
Defeat retry: A / Enter or R. Pause: Start / Esc.
Combat: right-stick direction cuts; LT + direction guards.
Keyboard combat: LMB cuts, RMB guards, mouse/arrows select attack direction.
Z / D-pad down draws or stows; I / View opens inventory; V / RS click changes view.
The interactive battlefield sequence is not a skippable movie.

IMPLEMENTATION BOUNDARIES
The battle's background duelists and archers are staged cinematic actors.
The three escape soldiers use the same physical contact/combat simulation as
live gameplay. This is a directed opening, not a simulated open battlefield.
Paired tackle/disarm/stab choreography uses the owned character rigs and capture
clips. It remains an animation/art polish target, not a claim of AAA completion.
Existing apartment audio, walk-only controls, hair-edge, turn-response, and
water-cache fixes are retained. Low/Medium/High/Ultra effects remain supported.

GRAPHICS AND CINEMATICS
The complete authored opening, paired ambush, NPC introduction and camera shots
play at every graphics level. No cinematic is removed for the RX 5300.
Graphics and Display retains the Cinematic preset, High/Ultra foliage distance,
High/Ultra environment effects, Ultra water, full reflection updates, cloud/fog
and shadow controls, and the independent 2K/4K character texture options.
These options remain available for more powerful computers. Lower levels reduce
rendering cost; they do not skip the story. Distant fallen soldiers now use
lighter meshes, with full-detail geometry kept farther away on High and Ultra.
The original nearby geometry and PBR maps remain intact.
Cinematic preserves your independently selected texture tier and automatic
resolution setting. For maximum output on capable hardware, choose Ultra
textures, Apply and restart; disable automatic image quality and select 100%
render scale if you want fixed native-resolution rendering. Ultra uses the
available source detail; it does not invent 4K detail for smaller source maps.
Compact character imports retain High surface maps while Ultra raises source
color detail. Auto can expand the shared texture pool on supported drivers
with ample startup headroom. Unknown or low-memory hardware keeps the existing
conservative budget. The graphics panel reports the resolved active pool.

KNOWN PLAYTEST LIMITATIONS
This is an early playable opening, not a claim of finished AAA art or animation.
Background battle actors are directed; nearby enemies use real combat. Tree
silhouettes, knight appearances and fallen-body poses still repeat. Terrain
composition, facial acting and paired choreography need more art refinement.
Long sessions can congest the road's crowd turnaround near the campsite.
Safe outward movement was corrected and the complete route passed natively;
travelers can still queue when the player occupies a tight turnaround.
The battlefield ground remains sparse and flat. Some banners and scenery can
remain absent when the shared texture pool fills on the 3 GB graphics card.
The tackle and recovery step are improved, but stiff arms, coarse hip movement
and occasional accessory overlap remain visible.
The RX 5300 (3 GB) remains below the desired frame-rate target: the tested
stationary battlefield view averaged about 20 FPS on the final detailed setup
and 28 FPS on the Medium environment/Balanced setup. Those are different
quality configurations, not a promise of smooth High/Ultra on this computer.
High/Ultra options remain available for faster machines. Normal keyboard/mouse
play is rehearsed natively; the controller mappings share the tested input
logic, but final controller feel still benefits from the player's own test.
