Valwood woodland, water and swimming update - Linux

Use Launch_Valwood.sh demo, editor, arena, joust or world. The five modes share
one rebuilt native executable and the reviewed shader bank. No Windows package
is prepared in this pass.

Graphics: press F10, or use Pause > Graphics. In Quality > Water choose Low,
Medium, High or Ultra, then Apply and save. These change real reflection work,
not swimming or collision. Low uses sky reflections and skips scene reflection
passes. Medium uses smaller mirrors and a shorter single-sample screen-space
reflection. High uses filtered reflections. Ultra allows full-resolution
mirrors and a longer reflection trace. The advanced reflection cap can reduce
the selected tier further; choosing a new water tier resets that cap to full.
Turning Water reflections off overrides every tier. Existing v1-v5 settings
load as High without changing saved files until Apply. New default is Medium.

The woodland changes use existing purchased vegetation and existing free
materials: bank boulders, low stones, fallen logs, roots, varied undergrowth,
local litter and distant tree groups. Terrain heights, water footprints,
quest records and the established swim routes are preserved. Existing solid
objects retain their movement blockers; new boulders/logs have blockers.

Four owned oak/beech imports now retain their source leaf transmission masks,
linear tints and view dependency. Other models retain their existing material
response. The river reflection compositor and roughness filtering are corrected.
A conservative sole-river support bound avoids reflection geometry pixels
that cannot be sampled, while retaining the full reflected sky and resolution.

First-person swim registration uses the captured head through the existing
entry/shore bridge. World body poses, weapon contacts and third-person meshes
stay authoritative. This registration removes the sampled detached shoulder
cuts, but the ordinary native review also shows that hands are outside the
view during the sampled surface stroke. First-person stroke presentation is
unfinished; the CPU cap-zero result is not proof of visible connected hands.

Remaining visual work includes irregular creek-bank shaping, stretched ground
detail and more varied canopy composition. Medium water can show stepped
reflections; High and Ultra filter them. Looking steeply down while treading
can still expose proximal arm caps in the software pose sweep. A moving native
review and its exact scope are recorded separately; an idle screenshot does
not certify the swim cycle.

On the RX 5300, water quality alone does not make the current maximum-detail
scene smooth. The Performance preset also reduces foliage and shadow cost.
All measured frame intervals and tier tradeoffs are retained in the review.

review/FINAL_REVIEW.json records visual acceptance, actual hardware timings,
swim/control checks and known limitations. review/CHANGESET.json binds the
exact reviewed source, binary, asset overlay, shaders and evidence used for
publication. This incremental update is not a AAA certification, a claim of
publisher-reference parity, or a guarantee of sustained frame rate.
